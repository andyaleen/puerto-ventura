# Farming System

Farming in **Puerto Ventura** provides players with a peaceful home base between adventures across the island. While exploration, restoration, and discovery drive the overall experience, the player's farm offers a place to slow down, establish routines, and create something uniquely their own.

Unlike traditional farming simulators where the farm is the primary focus, Puerto Ventura treats farming as one important part of a much larger world. The farm should feel personal and rewarding without becoming the dominant source of progression or income.

# Farming Philosophy

The player's farm is their home.

It is where they return after long expeditions into the jungle, mines, deserts, and mountains. It provides stability, food, income, crafting space, and opportunities for personalization, serving as a familiar refuge while the rest of the island gradually transforms.

The farm should complement exploration—not compete with it.

Players should be excited to return home after a day of adventure, tend their crops, organize resources, upgrade their property, and prepare for tomorrow's journey.

# The Homestead

The player begins with a small beachside cottage and a modest plot of land overlooking the ocean.

The homestead eventually expands to include:

- Crop fields
- Fruit trees
- Storage buildings
- Barns
- Animal pens
- Workshops
- Decorative gardens
- Outdoor gathering spaces

Expansion should happen gradually, allowing the farm to grow alongside the player's progress throughout the island.

Even in the late game, the farm should remain cozy rather than becoming an enormous industrial operation.

# Crop Farming

Players cultivate a variety of tropical fruits, vegetables, herbs, and specialty plants.

The basic farming loop remains familiar:

- Till soil
- Plant crops
- Water plants
- Care for growing fields
- Harvest produce
- Replant or rotate crops

Harvested crops can be:

- Sold for profit
- Used in cooking
- Given as gifts
- Used in crafting
- Contributed toward restoration projects
- Requested during quests

Farming should provide a relaxing rhythm between larger adventures.

# Regional Agriculture

Because Puerto Ventura is a tropical island, the game does not revolve around traditional four-season farming.

Instead, different regions of the island naturally support different crops.

Examples include:

- Beach fruits
- Jungle herbs
- Mountain berries
- Desert succulents
- Tropical vegetables
- Rare flowers

Unlocking new regions expands the variety of crops available to the player, encouraging exploration while keeping farming fresh throughout the game.

The emphasis should remain on clarity and discovery rather than complicated agricultural systems.

# Animals

Livestock becomes available after the island reaches certain restoration milestones.

Potential animals include:

- Chickens
- Goats
- Pigs
- Cows
- Ducks
- Tropical birds
- Island-specific wildlife

Animals provide resources such as milk, eggs, wool, feathers, and other products used for cooking, crafting, and commerce.

Animal care should remain simple and enjoyable without becoming overly demanding.

# Farm Progression

As players earn money and upgrade tools, the homestead gradually becomes more efficient.

Potential upgrades include:

- Improved irrigation
- Larger storage
- Better fencing
- Expanded crop fields
- Fruit orchards
- Greenhouses
- Decorative landscaping
- Higher-quality farm equipment

Each upgrade should improve convenience while preserving the cozy feel of maintaining a small island farm.

# Farming & the Economy

Farming is intended to be one of several reliable income sources.

It is important, but it should never overshadow the rest of the game.

Fishing should remain strong, eventually progressing to more scalable methods. Also allowing fishing for special, elusive fish for collections.

Mining provides valuable resources.

Exploration uncovers rare materials and artifacts.

Farming offers steady, dependable income while supporting cooking, gifting, crafting, and restoration.

Players should feel rewarded regardless of which activities they enjoy most.

# Integration with Other Systems

Farming naturally supports many other gameplay systems.

Crops can be:

- Cooked into meals
- Gifted to townspeople
- Processed into artisan goods
- Sold through restored businesses
- Used during festivals
- Donated for restoration projects
- Requested in character quests

This interconnected design helps ensure farming contributes meaningfully to the broader experience rather than existing in isolation.

# Design Principles

The farming system should follow several core principles:

- Keep the starting farm compact and approachable.
- Limit the number of crops available in Version 1 to maintain clarity.
- Unlock new crops gradually through exploration instead of seasonal changes.
- Favor meaningful progression over sheer farm size.
- Keep watering, harvesting, and replanting satisfying without becoming repetitive.
- Farming should support—not replace—fishing, exploration, mining, and restoration as viable paths for progression.

# The Farming Loop

The farming gameplay loop is intentionally relaxing and easy to understand.

**Plant** crops on the player's homestead.

↓

**Water and care** for growing plants.

↓

**Harvest** produce when mature.

↓

**Sell, cook, craft, gift, or contribute** crops toward restoration projects.

↓

**Upgrade** the farm and unlock new crops from newly restored regions.

↓

**Return home** after each island adventure to continue growing both the homestead and Puerto Ventura itself.

The farm should always feel like the player's personal sanctuary—a peaceful place to recharge between expeditions, reflect on the day's progress, and watch another small piece of Puerto Ventura flourish.
