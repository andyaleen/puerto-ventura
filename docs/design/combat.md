# Combat System

Combat in Puerto Ventura is designed to support exploration—not define it. While dangerous areas exist throughout the island, combat is never the primary focus of the game. The goal is to create moments of excitement, tension, and discovery while preserving the relaxing, cozy atmosphere that defines Puerto Ventura.

The player should never feel forced into constant fighting. Instead, combat serves as one of several gameplay pillars alongside farming, fishing, mining, building, exploration, and relationships. A player who prefers peaceful activities should be able to spend hours tending crops, restoring the town, or socializing, while players seeking adventure can venture into the island's more dangerous regions for greater rewards.

# Safe Zones

Several areas of the island are completely peaceful and contain no combat.

These include:

Beach
Beach Town
Idyllic City Center
Mountain
Player Homestead
Most restored public spaces

These locations are designed to feel safe, welcoming, and relaxing. Wildlife here is friendly, NPCs go about their daily routines, and players can focus on exploration, building, and social activities without interruption.

# Low Danger Areas

The first dangerous regions introduce players to conflict without overwhelming them.

Jungle

The jungle should feel wild rather than hostile.

Instead of constant enemies, players encounter:

Territorial monkeys
Snakes hiding in tall grass
Giant crabs
Aggressive birds
Wild boars
Carnivorous plants

Many obstacles are environmental rather than combat-based.

Examples include:

Dense vines
Fallen trees
Poison plants
Quicksand
Collapsed bridges
Ancient mechanisms
Tool-gated pathways

Players often solve problems using upgraded tools rather than weapons.

Combat is intentionally simple and forgiving.

Wildlife usually retreats after being frightened away instead of fighting to the death, reinforcing the idea that the player is exploring nature—not invading a battlefield.

# Medium Danger Areas

As players venture farther from civilization, the island becomes more hazardous.

Examples include:

Deep Jungle
Desert Cave


These locations introduce stronger creatures, environmental hazards, and longer expeditions.

Players begin relying more heavily on:

Better weapons
Improved armor
Healing food
Resource management

Combat becomes more strategic while remaining approachable.

Exploration remains the primary objective.

# High Danger Areas

The island's most mysterious regions contain its greatest dangers.

These include:

Desert
Cursed Region
Volcano
Ancient underground ruins
Late-game story locations

Here, players encounter significantly more dangerous enemies and environmental threats.

Combat evolves into a fuller system that rewards preparation and progression.

Players are expected to upgrade:

Weapons
Armor
Shields
Charms
Potions
Food buffs

These regions should feel like true adventures that require planning before entering.

Progression Philosophy

Combat should scale naturally alongside the player's overall progression.

Rather than introducing every mechanic immediately, Puerto Ventura gradually teaches players through increasingly dangerous environments.

The progression is currently envisioned as:

# Tier 0 – Peaceful Exploration

No combat.

Gameplay revolves around:

Exploration
Gathering
Building
Fishing
Farming
Puzzle solving
Tool progression

Obstacles are environmental rather than hostile.

Examples include blocked paths, broken bridges, dense vegetation, and ancient mechanisms that require upgraded tools.

# Tier 1 – Light Survival

Very light combat.

Players use simple tools or basic weapons to scare away wildlife and defend themselves from minor threats.

The emphasis remains on exploration rather than fighting.

Enemies are forgiving, encounters are brief, and most wildlife simply wants to protect its territory.

# Tier 2 – Adventure

Moderate combat appears in caves, mines, and deeper wilderness.

Players unlock stronger equipment, learn enemy attack patterns, and begin making meaningful equipment choices.

Exploration and treasure remain the primary reward.

Combat simply becomes another challenge to overcome.

# Tier 3 – Expedition

Late-game areas demand preparation.

The Desert, Volcano, Cursed Region, and ancient ruins introduce tougher enemies, larger encounters, environmental hazards, and optional boss fights.

Players who invest in upgraded weapons, armor, food, and crafting gain a noticeable advantage.

These areas represent the most dangerous places on Puerto Ventura and contain some of the game's rarest resources and greatest secrets.

Weapons & Equipment

Equipment progression should feel rewarding without becoming overly complex.

Current categories include:

Basic tools that can double as improvised weapons
Swords
Spears
Clubs
Bows (potential)
Shields
Light armor
Heavy armor
Boots
Accessories and charms

Equipment upgrades primarily increase survivability and unlock access to more dangerous regions rather than encouraging players to grind combat.

# Enemy Design Philosophy

Enemies should reflect the island's ecosystems instead of feeling like generic monsters.

Examples include:

Territorial wildlife
Venomous jungle creatures
Desert predators
Cave-dwelling insects
Living plants
Ancient guardians
Cursed spirits
Volcanic creatures

Each biome should have its own distinct enemies that reinforce the identity of the environment.

# Overarching notes

Combat should remain largely optional for players who prefer the cozy aspects of Puerto Ventura.

Many restoration projects, friendships, businesses, and upgrades can be completed with little or no fighting. However, players willing to brave dangerous regions will gain access to rare resources, powerful equipment, hidden lore, and optional storylines that reward adventurous play without making combat a mandatory focus of the experience.

The overarching philosophy is simple: combat exists to make exploration more exciting—not to replace it. Players should feel like explorers venturing into the unknown, not heroes clearing endless waves of enemies.
