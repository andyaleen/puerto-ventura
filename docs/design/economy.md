# Economy System

The economy in **Puerto Ventura** exists to support the game's central goal: rebuilding the island. Money is not the ultimate objective (but is possible), nor is accumulating massive stockpiles of resources. Instead, the economy provides players with meaningful decisions about how to invest in themselves, their home, and the future of Puerto Ventura.

Every dollar earned and every resource gathered should contribute toward tangible progress. When the player spends money, they should immediately see the island become more vibrant, accessible, and alive.

# Economy Philosophy

Unlike many life simulation games where wealth eventually loses its purpose, Puerto Ventura is designed around continuous investment.

Players earn money through productive activities and spend it on improvements that permanently expand gameplay opportunities.

The economy should encourage players to ask:

- Should I upgrade my pickaxe?
- Should I restore the harbor first?
- Should I save for a greenhouse?
- Should I invest in reopening the coffee shop?
- Should I buy better equipment before exploring the volcano?

There should rarely be a "correct" answer, allowing players to choose the pace and direction of their own progression.

# Core Currencies

The prototype currently tracks several core resources:

- Money
- Current Day
- Basic gathered resources (wood, stone, shells, fiber, etc.)

As development continues, additional resource categories will naturally emerge through exploration, mining, farming, fishing, and crafting.

The economy should remain simple enough that players always understand what they own and what they are working toward.

# Sources of Income

Players earn money through a variety of interconnected activities.

Primary income sources include:

- Gathering natural resources
- Fishing (major income source throughout the game)
- Farming crops and orchards
- Mining ore, gems, and rare minerals
- Selling artisan goods
- Completing restoration contracts
- Discovering valuable artifacts
- Trading with reopened businesses
- Late-game tourism and specialty industries

Each playstyle should feel viable, allowing players to naturally gravitate toward the activities they enjoy most.

# Spending & Resource Sinks

Resources should continuously flow back into improving Puerto Ventura rather than accumulating indefinitely.

Major investments include:

- Tool crafting and upgrades
- Purchasing equipment
- Expanding the player's home
- Building workshops and enterprises
- Restoration projects
- Unlocking new infrastructure
- Business improvements
- Decorative customization
- Expedition supplies

The largest and most satisfying expenditures should always result in visible changes to the world.

# Restoration as the Primary Money Sink

The island itself is the greatest investment.

Nearly every district contains restoration projects that require combinations of money, crafted materials, and gathered resources.

Examples include:

- Rebuilding bridges
- Repairing docks
- Restoring irrigation systems
- Renovating abandoned homes
- Reopening shops
- Repairing museums
- Restoring parks and gardens
- Constructing public buildings

Every completed project permanently improves Puerto Ventura and often unlocks entirely new gameplay opportunities.

Money should feel like an investment in the island rather than something to simply accumulate.

# Businesses & Trade

As buildings are restored, new businesses begin operating.

Each reopened business expands the local economy by introducing:

- New items
- New services
- Better equipment
- Additional recipes
- New quests
- Improved buying and selling opportunities

The economy naturally grows alongside the town itself.

A thriving Puerto Ventura becomes capable of supporting larger projects, attracting visitors, and creating entirely new opportunities for both the player and the community.

# Resource Variety

Rather than encouraging players to endlessly grind a single profitable activity, progression should reward diverse playstyles.

Different restoration projects should require different combinations of materials.

Examples might include:

- Lumber from the forest
- Stone from the mines
- Fish for local restaurants
- Clay for brickmaking
- Coral from the coast
- Herbs from the jungle
- Glass made from desert sand

This encourages players to explore every biome while keeping the gathering loop varied and rewarding.

# Economic Balance

The economy should be generous enough to feel relaxing while still encouraging thoughtful decision-making.

Design goals include:

- Early restoration projects should be affordable after a short gathering session.
- Players should regularly feel like they can afford meaningful upgrades.
- Multiple income sources should remain viable throughout the game.
- Resource requirements should encourage exploration rather than repetitive grinding.
- Costs should teach players to diversify resources without requiring spreadsheets or excessive optimization.
- Money should always have meaningful uses, even in the late game.

The objective is to create satisfying progression rather than financial simulation.

# Visible Progress

One of the guiding principles of Puerto Ventura's economy is:

**Visible spend → visible world change.**

Whenever players invest resources into the island, they should immediately notice the results.

A repaired bridge opens a new path.

A restored shop fills with customers.

A rebuilt fountain begins flowing again.

A renovated home welcomes a new family.

A restored harbor fills with fishing boats.

Every major purchase should make Puerto Ventura feel more alive.

# Long-Term Vision

The economy should reinforce the game's core message: prosperity is measured by the health of the community, not by the size of the player's bank account.

By the end of the game, the player has likely earned a considerable fortune, but the true reward is walking through a thriving island where bustling markets, restored landmarks, successful businesses, and happy residents all stand as visible proof of how every resource gathered and every dollar invested helped bring Puerto Ventura back to life.
