# Progression System

Progression in **Puerto Ventura** is centered around one guiding principle:

**Every meaningful action should help bring the island back to life.**

Rather than focusing on leveling up a character or accumulating wealth for its own sake, players progress by restoring Puerto Ventura district by district. Each completed project makes the island visibly more vibrant, expands gameplay opportunities, introduces new characters, and uncovers another piece of its forgotten history.

The restoration of the island is the backbone of the entire experience. Every other gameplay system—farming, fishing, mining, crafting, exploration, combat, and relationships—exists to support this larger goal.

# Progression Philosophy

The player should constantly feel like they are making visible progress.

Instead of unlocking abstract experience levels or invisible stat increases, nearly every milestone should produce an immediate change that the player can see, hear, or interact with.

Examples include:

- A bridge is rebuilt.
- A shop reopens.
- New NPCs move into town.
- Flowers bloom in a restored plaza.
- Fishing boats return to the harbor.
- Jungle paths become accessible.
- Music changes as districts recover.
- New festivals begin taking place.

Puerto Ventura itself serves as the player's progress bar.

# Restoration: The Core Progression Loop

Every major restoration project should accomplish multiple goals simultaneously.

Completing a restoration may:

- Change the appearance of the world.
- Reopen businesses and services.
- Bring former residents back to the island.
- Unlock new quests.
- Open new paths and regions.
- Introduce new mechanics.
- Reveal pieces of the island's history.
- Expand the local economy.
- Create new opportunities for friendships and romance.

Players should always feel that restoration projects are among the most rewarding milestones in the game.

# Layered Progression

Rather than relying on a single progression system, Puerto Ventura layers several forms of progression that naturally support one another.

## Tools

Upgrading equipment allows players to gather resources more efficiently while reaching areas that were previously inaccessible.

Examples include:

- Better axes clearing dense jungle.
- Stronger pickaxes breaking volcanic rock.
- Improved fishing rods reaching deeper waters.
- Better climbing equipment accessing mountain paths.

Tool progression expands exploration rather than simply increasing efficiency.

## Resources & Economy

Gathering, farming, fishing, mining, and crafting provide the money and materials needed to restore Puerto Ventura.

Resources become investments rather than collectibles.

Players continually decide where to spend their limited materials to create the greatest impact on the island.

## Restoration

Each restored district permanently changes the world.

Entire neighborhoods evolve from abandoned ruins into thriving communities filled with shops, homes, businesses, and residents.

Restoration represents the single largest source of progression throughout the game.

## Social Progression

Relationships deepen as the island recovers.

Characters become happier, pursue their dreams, open businesses, build families, and participate more actively in community life.

Many friendship events, romance options, and personal storylines only become available once the locations that matter to those characters have been restored.

As Puerto Ventura grows, so do its people.

## World Progression

The island gradually expands as restoration and story milestones are completed.

New districts, hidden ruins, transportation routes, mysterious civilizations, and late-game story content become accessible only after the player has meaningfully contributed to rebuilding Puerto Ventura.

The player's understanding of the island grows alongside its physical restoration.

# Soft & Hard Progression Gates

Puerto Ventura uses both soft and hard progression gates to guide exploration while preserving player freedom.

## Soft Gates

Soft gates encourage players to return later without completely preventing exploration.

Examples include:

- Better fishing spots requiring upgraded rods.
- Richer mining deposits needing stronger tools.
- Faster farming through improved equipment.
- Cosmetic improvements as towns become livelier.
- New merchants and businesses offering better goods.
- Higher-quality resources becoming available.

These upgrades reward progression without forcing it.

## Hard Gates

Some areas remain inaccessible until specific conditions are met.

Examples include:

- Dense vine walls blocking jungle passages.
- Broken bridges requiring restoration.
- Landslides blocking mountain trails.
- Cliffs requiring climbing equipment.
- Ancient doors needing special technology or artifacts.
- Desert aqueducts requiring reconstruction before crossing.
- Locked neighborhoods that only reopen after restoration milestones.
- Ancient energy barriers that can only be disabled after rediscovering lost technology.

These gates create anticipation and encourage players to revisit earlier regions with new capabilities.

# Story Progression

The mystery of Puerto Ventura unfolds gradually alongside the island's restoration.

Rather than delivering the entire narrative through cutscenes, the game rewards exploration and rebuilding with new discoveries.

As players restore districts and unlock new regions, they uncover:

- Forgotten journals.
- Ancient ruins.
- Hidden laboratories.
- Tribal history.
- Advanced technology.
- Long-lost infrastructure.
- The truth behind the island's decline.

Every major revelation should feel earned through gameplay rather than simply handed to the player.

# Visual Progression

One of Puerto Ventura's defining features is that progression is always visible.

Players should be able to stand in the town square and immediately recognize how far they've come.

Examples include:

- Empty storefronts becoming thriving businesses.
- Dirt paths becoming landscaped roads.
- Dead gardens blooming again.
- More NPCs walking through town.
- New boats filling the harbor.
- Wildlife returning to restored ecosystems.
- New music and ambient sounds reflecting a healthier island.

The island should feel increasingly alive with every restoration milestone.

# Prototype Progression Hooks

Several early restoration projects are designed to immediately demonstrate the game's philosophy.

**Beach Harbor**

- Restore the fountain.
- Repair the docks.
- Reopen the fish market.
- Open the café.
- Bring fishing boats back to the harbor.

**Idyllic City Center**

- Restore the central fountain.
- Reopen Town Hall.
- Renovate the museum.
- Revive the marketplace.
- Replant the public gardens.

These projects act as visible proof that the player's actions permanently improve Puerto Ventura.

# Anti-Goals

Avoid invisible experience systems where players level up without seeing meaningful changes in the world.

Avoid unlocking every biome too quickly before earlier districts have had time to feel alive and worth revisiting.

# Long-Term Vision

By the end of the game, progression should feel less like completing a checklist and more like watching an entire civilization come back to life.

The player doesn't simply unlock stronger tools or earn more money—they transform Puerto Ventura from a neglected island into a thriving paradise. Every restored building, every returning resident, every newly opened path, and every mystery uncovered stands as a permanent reminder of the journey. The island itself becomes the clearest measure of the player's success, making progression feel tangible, memorable, and deeply rewarding.
